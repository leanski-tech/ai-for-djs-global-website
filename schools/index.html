How to actually learn this

DJ Schools Directory

Formal courses, community programs and the route most working DJs actually took. Judge any of them on the same four questions below.

Route one
Community and youth programs

Libraries, arts nonprofits and after-school music programs. Cheapest real access to gear and a room, and often taught by DJs still gigging. Ask your local arts council what's funded this year.

Route two
Private lessons with a working DJ

The fastest correction of bad habits. Two hours a month with someone who plays the rooms you want is worth more than a term of theory. Expect $50–150 an hour.

Route three
Music schools and colleges

Structured, credentialed, and strongest for production and audio engineering rather than floor craft. Worth it if you want the studio skills alongside the decks.

Route four
Crews, mentors and battles

How most of the greats learned, including the founder of this site. You get standards, criticism and a scene. It costs nothing and asks for everything.

Route five
Online courses

Good for technique drills you can repeat, bad for reading a room. Use them for scratching, gain staging and library organisation, not for learning taste.

Route six
The unpaid warm-up slot

Ask a promoter for the first hour and take it seriously. Playing to twelve people at 9pm teaches patience and programming that no course can.

Four questions before you pay

01

Who is teaching, and are they still getting booked? Ask where they played last month.

02

How many hours are your hands actually on gear, versus watching a screen?

03

Does it end with you playing in front of people? A course with no gig at the end is half a course.

04

Is the business side covered at all? If not, that's what the Playbook is for.
