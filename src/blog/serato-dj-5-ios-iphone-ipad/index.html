Serato DJ 5.0 and the New iPhone and iPad App: What DJs Can Do Now

Serato on your iPhone or iPad is officially here—and I’m getting my music ready to try it this weekend.

On October 20, 2026, Serato introduced Serato DJ for iOS, bringing its DJ software to iPhone and iPad. The launch accompanies Serato DJ 5.0 for computers, connecting desktop preparation with a new mobile way to mix. Official announcement.

I’ve been uploading my music so I can use my iPad or iPhone this weekend. Before I put it to work, here’s what the software actually offers.

What’s New in Serato DJ 5.0 on Your Computer?

The desktop update includes several changes worth knowing:

  • Lucid Beatgrids: A new analysis engine follows tempo changes, including tracks with live drumming. That could be particularly useful when preparing funk, soul, disco, and other music with a moving tempo.
  • Open Streaming: You can stay logged into multiple streaming providers and use them in the same set.
  • Cue Loops: Trigger a cue point and activate a loop with one pad press or click.
  • iOS Transfer: Move songs and crates to the mobile app with cue points, beatgrids, and track information.
  • Library improvements: The update adds M3U playlist importing and improves searching and responsiveness for large libraries.

These are documented features of the desktop release; how well they fit my workflow is something I’ll evaluate through use. Serato DJ Pro 5.0 release notes.

What Can Serato Do on iPhone and iPad?

You can mix directly on the touchscreen using virtual decks, EQ controls, and a crossfader. You can also connect supported DJ hardware.

The mobile app includes Serato Stems, which separates vocals, drums, bass, and melody. That opens up possibilities such as mixing an instrumental under another track’s vocal. Depending on your license, the app also offers Stems FX, advanced performance pads, key shifting, Key Sync, and DVS for compatible turntable setups.

The full feature set includes up to eight cue points, eight Cue Loops, and eight sampler slots. Features and available slot counts vary between the free and paid versions. Mobile release notes.

Bringing My Music to the App

For DJs with an existing Serato library, iOS Transfer is a major part of the appeal. Serato DJ Pro or Lite 5.0 or later can send crates and music to the mobile app while preserving preparation work.

There are other ways to load music, too. The app can import songs or folders through Apple’s Files app, including files from connected cloud services such as iCloud, Dropbox, or Google Drive. Those services require their apps to be installed and signed in. Serato’s music-import guide.

Streaming and Recording

Serato DJ for iOS supports Apple Music, Beatport, SoundCloud, Spotify, and TIDAL.

Recording is available in the paid version when using local or transferred music. You cannot record a set while streaming tracks are loaded. Also, some desktop features—including Serato Video, Serato Flip, custom MIDI mapping, and Ableton Link—remain laptop-only. Serato’s mobile FAQ.

Is It Free?

There is a free version with basic mixing tools, Stems, effects, cues, samples, Auto Loop, and library transfer.

As of October 20, 2026, Serato lists these paid options in US dollars:

  • Serato DJ for iOS outright: $149.
  • Serato DJ Suite subscription: $14.99 per month.
  • Serato DJ Suite outright: $499.

Suite includes the mobile app, Serato DJ Pro, all DJ Pro expansions, and Serato Studio. Paid mobile features include recording, advanced effects, and DVS support. Official pricing and comparison.

Check Your Device and Controller

The minimum operating system is iOS 18.6 or iPadOS 18.6, although Serato recommends version 26 or later. Its recommended devices include iPhone 15 or later, iPad Air fourth generation, or iPad tenth generation. System requirements.

Controller compatibility needs a separate check. Not every device supported by desktop Serato works on iOS, and some require a paid license. The mobile app currently uses two decks, even with a four-deck controller. Supported hardware.

Your cable and power setup also matter. USB-powered controllers may need a powered hub or adapter. Connection guide.

What I’ll Be Testing This Weekend

I’m interested in how quickly I can find songs, how comfortable mixing feels on each screen, and how the setup performs during an extended session.

If you’re testing it too, Serato recommends configuring Focus or Do Not Disturb to prevent calls and notifications from interrupting playback. Performance guidance.

For now, I’m getting my library ready. After the weekend, I’ll have something more useful than a feature list: my own experience using it.
