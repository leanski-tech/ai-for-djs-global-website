rekordbox Just Made an AI Mixing Aid Free for Every DJ

rekordbox 7.2.16 gives Free-plan users access to AI vocal-position detection, helping DJs see vocal sections before they collide in a mix.

AlphaTheta released rekordbox 7.2.16 on July 9, 2026. One small line in the update could make a useful difference for DJs: AI detection of vocal positions is now available on every rekordbox subscription plan, including the Free plan.

This is the kind of AI development DJs should pay attention to. It does not select the next record, read the crowd, or decide when to mix. It gives the DJ clearer information and leaves the creative decision in human hands.

What Vocal Position Detection Does

When rekordbox analyzes a track, its vocal-position feature identifies sections where vocals appear and displays them on the waveform. AlphaTheta says the system uses a deep-learning algorithm originally developed with the technology company Qosmo.

The feature has existed in rekordbox since 2020, but it was previously tied to higher subscription access. With version 7.2.16, rekordbox says the analysis and display are available even on its Free plan.

That makes the real news accessibility, not automation. More DJs can now use the visual information without upgrading solely for this feature.

Three Practical Ways DJs Can Use It

1. Prevent vocal-on-vocal clashes

A DJ can look ahead on the waveform and see that the incoming track's vocal begins while the current singer or rapper is still active. The DJ can wait, loop an instrumental section, choose a different cue point, or select another track.

The software provides the warning. The DJ still decides what sounds right.

2. Prepare cleaner cue points

During set preparation, vocal markers can help DJs place hot cues near intros, hooks, verses, and instrumental spaces. That may reduce guesswork when preparing unfamiliar edits or a large batch of music.

The markers should be treated as a visual reference, not a substitute for listening to the full track.

3. Plan blends more quickly

Open-format, wedding, radio, and event DJs often work with songs that have short intros or dense vocals. Seeing vocal sections across the waveform can help identify possible transition windows faster, especially when preparation time is limited.

It can also help a DJ recognize when a transition needs more care instead of encouraging a risky automatic blend.

Limitations and Compatibility

The latest rekordbox version is required. The current FAQ identifies version 7.2.16 as the point when the feature became available on the Free plan.

On Windows, rekordbox says vocal-position detection requires a processor that supports AVX instructions.

AI analysis can be wrong. DJs should confirm every marker by listening before relying on it during a performance.

This article is based on official rekordbox documentation, researched by DJ Leanskee. The feature was not independently benchmarked across genres, live recordings, acapellas, or heavily processed vocals.

Streaming-service restrictions and other rekordbox functions may vary by plan. Free access to vocal-position detection does not mean every rekordbox feature is free.

The DJ Stays in Control

This feature does not understand the room, the event, the culture, or why one lyric belongs after another. It cannot replace timing, taste, technical skill, or crowd awareness.

Used properly, AI vocal detection acts like an extra visual layer in the DJ's preparation. It can point out where vocals may appear. The DJ decides whether to blend, cut, loop, scratch, echo out, or let the record breathe.

That is a useful role for AI in DJing: handle a narrow analysis task and give the human more information.

A Safe Next Step

After updating to rekordbox 7.2.16 or later, analyze three tracks you already know well with vocal analysis enabled. Compare the displayed vocal sections with what you hear. If the markers are reliable for those tracks, use them to practice one transition — but keep listening instead of mixing by sight alone.
