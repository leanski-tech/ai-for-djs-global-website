AI, DJing, and the Never-Ending Debate About Technology

I have heard this same warning my whole life.

"This is going to ruin DJing."

They said it about CDs. They said it about MP3s. They said it about laptops, controllers, sync buttons, streaming, and stems.

Now they are saying it about AI.

You know, I get it. I really do. But I have been in this culture a long time, and I have seen this story play out over and over again.

I Come From the Crates

I came up in Boston. I am a founding member of the Floorlords, together since 1981. I have been living Hip Hop for over 45 years and DJing for over 30 years.

Rusty the Toe Jam and Larry Dee taught me how to DJ back in my early years. Real teachers. Real records. Real work.

Back then, if you did not carry it, you did not play it. Crates were heavy. Needles broke. Records got scratched. And beatmatching took real time to learn. Nobody handed you that. You earned it in rooms where people could tell, right away, if you were faking it.

So when I talk about new tools, I am not talking from the outside. I lived the old way first.

Every Tool Started an Argument

There was a time when a DJ showing up with a laptop was called a cheater.

There was a time when MP3s were not "real" DJing.

Sync buttons started fights that are still going on today.

And you know what happened? Most DJs found a way to use the tool and keep doing the work. What scared everybody at first just became part of the job.

That does not mean every new tool is good. It does not. It means we have a history of fearing the change before we learn what to do with it.

I know about starting over, too. I once gave away all my gear and my records, and my old records got thrown in the trash. I started again on a borrowed setup. So I do not take tools lightly. I know what it feels like to lose the things you built your craft on.

A Tool Does Not Have Taste

This is the part people miss.

A controller does not teach you how to read a crowd.

Software does not give you taste.

Stems do not make a good mashup by themselves. Anybody can press the button. Not everybody knows what to do next.

AI is the same way.

AI can help you write, organize, do research, make a flyer, plan your content. Real help. But it cannot feel a room shift. It cannot know the exact second to take a chance on a record. It cannot build trust with a dance floor, song after song, the way a real DJ does.

That part is still you. That part will always be you.

I Understand Why Some DJs Push Back

Let me be honest about the other side, because it is real.

A lot of us spent years learning skills that a machine can now do in seconds. Beatmatching took years. Finding an acapella took digging, and trading, and luck. Now stems can pull a vocal off a track before your coffee gets cold.

So the fear is not really about a button.

The fear is about losing something you worked hard for. Something valuable. Something that made you who you are.

That is a fair fear. And it is a conversation we should have out loud, not by judging each other for using a tool, or for not using it.

What AI Actually Does for Me

For me, AI is not playing my sets. AI is not picking my music and it is not reading my crowd.

AI helps me with the stuff around the music.

The emails. The follow ups. The contracts. The posts. The website copy. The hours at a desk that take me away from the music and the people.

I did not get into this because I love paperwork. I got into this because I love music, and I love what music does to a room full of people.

If a tool gives me back time for that, I am going to look at it. With my eyes open, but I am going to look.

You Are Already Using It, You Know

Here is the truth nobody wants to say in these debates.

Most DJs already use AI every day and do not think twice about it.

The streaming app that suggests your next track. The platform that decides who sees your post. The stem tool in your software. That is all AI working in the background.

So the question is not, do you use AI?

The question is, are you using it on purpose, with your own judgment in charge?

What Will Make a Great DJ Now

If software keeps doing more of the technical work, then what is left is the stuff that always mattered most.

  • Taste.
  • Reading the room.
  • Telling a story with music.
  • Taking a risk when it would be safer not to.
  • Connecting with people, for real.

A playlist can have all the right songs in the wrong order and the night dies. An algorithm can make a clean transition and nobody feels a thing.

Technology can do a process. It cannot have a point of view. That is yours.

Do Not Let the Tool Think for You

Now, the warning, because there is one.

Do not get lazy with it. I mean that.

Just because AI suggests a track does not mean it is the right track for that room, that night. Just because it writes something fast does not mean it sounds like you.

Use it like an assistant. Check its work. Keep your own voice. Keep learning your craft the hard way, too, because that is where your instincts come from.

The DJs who will do well with this are not the ones who hand everything over. They are the ones who stay in charge of it.

There Is Room for Both

Loving vinyl does not make you old. Using AI does not make you fake.

A lot of us still love manual beatmatching. A lot of us still dig. That craft does not lose value just because a new tool showed up. If anything, real craft stands out even more now.

I still buy records myself. Not for my regular sets. My everyday setup is Serato, and that is what I play on. I buy them so I have them ready for those special vinyl events, when the night calls for the real thing.

It does not have to be tradition or technology. It can be both. Hip Hop itself started that way. Two turntables and a mixer were never made to do what DJs did with them. We took the tool and made culture out of it. That is who we are.

My Last Word on It

DJing has changed with every generation. Every generation got a new tool, had the same fight, and had to decide what it really values.

AI is just the newest chapter in that same story.

The DJs who make it will not be the ones who run from every new thing. And they will not be the ones who copy every trend either.

They will be the ones who stay curious. Who test things for themselves. Who remember that no software can replace taste, judgment, and a real connection with people through music.

Because at the end of the night, the dance floor does not care how you made your flyer, or what program helped you write your post.

The dance floor cares about one thing.

Did you give them a night they will remember?

Build. Teach. Legacy.
