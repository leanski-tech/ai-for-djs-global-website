DJ Equipment Guide

What to buy at each stage, and what to skip. No affiliate maths — this is the order a working DJ actually spends money in.

Stage one · learning
Two channels and headphones

A two-channel controller with a real crossfader, closed-back headphones you can wear for four hours, and one external drive for your library. That's it.

Skip: speakers, a mixer you don't need yet, anything with more than four channels.

What to get $250 – $600 all in
Pioneer DJ DDJ-FLX4 controller Numark Mixstream Pro (no laptop) Audio-Technica ATH-M50x headphones Shop controllers at Sweetwater
Stage two · first paid gigs
Sound you can carry alone

Two powered 12" tops, two stands, a wireless handheld mic, and a hard case for everything. One person, two trips. If you can't load it alone at 2am, it's the wrong rig.

Skip: lighting rigs, subs, a second laptop.

What to get $1,200 – $2,200
QSC K12.2 or JBL EON712 tops Shure BLX24/PG58 wireless mic Odyssey / Gator hard cases Speaker stands & cabling
Stage three · weddings and corporate
Redundancy is the product

A sub, a spare mic, a spare cable of every kind, a backup controller in the car and your set on two USBs. Clients aren't paying for your taste at this tier — they're paying for nothing going wrong.

Add: uplighting only when clients keep asking for it.

What to get $2,500 – $5,000
QSC KS112 or RCF SUB 705 sub Backup controller (keep the old one) Chauvet DJ uplighting Spare cable kit
Stage four · clubs
Learn the house gear

Clubs supply the CDJs and the mixer, so what you own matters less than what you can walk up to cold. Practise on the standard club layout, carry your own headphones, USBs and a needle if there are decks.

Skip: buying club-grade players before you have club-grade bookings.

What to get Practise, don't buy
Pioneer CDJ / DJM range (know the layout) Rane Twelve & Seventy-Two rekordbox export prep Hourly practice rooms & rentals
01

The bag that saves the night

Gaffer tape. Two extension runs. A power strip. Spare XLR and TRS. A ground-lift adapter. Batteries for the mic. A headphone adapter. A phone charger and a USB battery. Zip ties. A pen that works. Every one of these has rescued a paid gig, and together they cost less than one cancelled booking.
