Serato DJ Is Finally on iPhone and iPad. Here's What Happened When I Tried It at a Gig.

I've been DJing long enough to know one thing: the equipment doesn't care that you have a gig to play. It either works or it doesn't. And when it doesn't, you better have another way to keep the music going.

I was DJing the Sunshine People's Market, a thrift-shop market event, and I wanted to try the new Serato DJ app on my iPhone and iPad. I showed up ready to use it, but I couldn't get the setup working the way I needed. Luckily, I brought my laptop.

Instead of spending the event troubleshooting, I switched over and DJed from my laptop. The music kept going. I figured out the mobile problem later.

The part I didn't understand: Serato's mobile license

Serato DJ for iOS launched in October 2026. The app is free to download, and it has a free mode, but that doesn't mean every controller, mixer, or professional feature is unlocked. Serato says some hardware—including certain DVS mixers and interfaces—requires Serato DJ Suite to connect. If your gear shows License Required, the issue may be the license rather than the cable.

Here's the important distinction: Serato DJ Suite unlocks the full iOS experience and also includes Serato DJ Pro on the computer. There is also a one-time Serato DJ for iOS license for the mobile app, but it does not unlock desktop Serato DJ Pro. A standalone Serato DJ Pro license or hardware unlock on your laptop does not automatically mean you have all the iOS features.

That was the lesson I took from the Sunshine People's Market: don't assume that because Serato works on your laptop, the same setup will be unlocked on your phone or iPad.

How to unlock Serato DJ for iPhone or iPad

  1. Install Serato DJ for iOS from the App Store.
  2. Open the app and sign in to your Serato account.
  3. If you already own or subscribe to Serato DJ Suite, sign in with that same account so your entitlement can activate.
  4. Otherwise, tap Upgrade to review a Suite subscription or the standalone iOS license. Check which option supports your hardware before paying.
  5. Connect your controller or mixer and test actual audio output, headphones, and controls—not just whether the app opens.

Serato says you need an internet connection to log in and activate the license. Once activated, you can DJ offline with tracks transferred to the device; streaming music still requires internet.

Don't forget hardware, cables, and power

Not every piece of hardware that works with Serato DJ Pro on a laptop works with Serato DJ for iOS. Check Serato's iOS hardware list first. Some gear that depends on computer audio drivers is not supported.

For USB-C iPhones and iPads, use the correct USB cable or a powered USB-C hub when needed. For Lightning devices, Serato recommends Apple's Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter. A controller that needs USB power may not get enough power from the phone alone.

The minimum supported operating systems are iOS 18.6 or iPadOS 18.6. Serato recommends newer devices and OS versions for the best performance.

My five-minute pre-gig checklist

  • Open Serato DJ for iOS before leaving home and confirm the license is active.
  • Check your exact controller or mixer against the supported hardware list.
  • Connect the cables, power, headphones, and speakers, and play a full test track.
  • Transfer the music you need locally if venue Wi-Fi or cell service may be unreliable.
  • Bring a backup. For me, that laptop saved the gig.

What I learned

I love seeing DJ technology move forward. Being able to DJ from an iPhone or iPad is a big deal, especially for travel, smaller events, and lightweight setups. But I learned not to test a brand-new workflow for the first time in front of a crowd.

At the Sunshine People's Market, the mobile setup didn't work for me. I didn't let that stop the event. I used my laptop, kept the music moving, and dealt with the technical problem afterward. That's part of being a working DJ: know your gear, have a backup, and keep the people dancing.

— DJ Leanskee
